Facing the Tesla Model Y, the question of the best Chinese alternative comes up regularly in car comparisons published in France. Several manufacturers from China now offer family electric SUVs positioned on the same segment, with one model concentrating most of the direct comparisons: the Xpeng G6. This guide gathers the available reference points to place this rival, without claiming to reproduce a road test carried out by the editorial team.

This search for an alternative is not only about the spec sheet. Several published tests note that the American manufacturer's image now weighs on some buyers' decisions, alongside price or range. This factor, difficult to quantify, partly explains why comparisons dedicated to the Tesla Model Y's Chinese rivals have multiplied recently in the specialist press.

## The Xpeng G6, the benchmark among Chinese electric SUVs

The Xpeng G6 is the model most frequently pitted against the Tesla Model Y in the French motoring press. Its family SUV footprint and fastback body echo styling codes close to those of the American model, to the point that several tests highlight an acknowledged inspiration in the overall design and the continuous light bar at the front.

This stylistic closeness does not mean full equivalence. The two vehicles differ in country of manufacture, electrical architecture and software ecosystem. The Xpeng G6 is built in China, while the French-market Tesla Model Y comes from the brand's German plant, a distinction with concrete consequences for eligibility for public incentives, detailed further in this guide.

### An interior geared more towards screens and rear comfort

Published comparative tests describe a Xpeng G6 cabin with an approach close to that of the Tesla Model Y, with functions largely centralised on the main screen. The rear bench is described as offering generous legroom and a more enveloping seat than the American model, with more reclinable backrests. The Tesla Model Y, for its part, keeps a dedicated rear entertainment screen, absent from the Xpeng G6.

## Range and charging: what an 800-volt architecture changes

Range remains a central criterion when comparing two family electric SUVs. Published measurements place both vehicles in a similar range, around 400 kilometres in real mixed use depending on test conditions, with the Xpeng G6 version carrying a battery announced with a higher usable capacity than the Tesla Model Y tested for comparison.

The most documented difference concerns fast charging. The Xpeng G6 relies on an **800-volt architecture**, which lets it go from 10 to 80 percent charge in around twenty minutes according to one test, a time brought down to roughly 12 to 14 minutes in the most recent measurements following a battery update. The Tesla Model Y, whose architecture remains at 400 volts, shows slightly longer fast-charging times outside its own Supercharger network, where its 800-volt compatibility is not activated.

The charging network is precisely where the advantage changes sides. Tesla customers benefit from simplified access and preferential rates on the brand's Superchargers, a dense network across France, while a Xpeng G6 owner depends on shared public chargers, whose power and reliability vary more.

## Cabin, equipment and onboard technology

Beyond the rear comfort already mentioned, the best-equipped Xpeng G6 versions include standard semi-autonomous driving and parking aids, as well as large-diameter wheels, features the Tesla Model Y does not systematically offer at the same trim level. The electric tailgate, which opens and closes automatically, is also noted as a positive point by testers.

This standard equipment matters in the overall comparison, particularly when the price gap between two close versions narrows to a few hundred euros. A useful comparison grid always ties the actual equipment level to the specific version, rather than to the model's generic name.

## Price and the ecological bonus: the gap to know before comparing

French prices for the Xpeng G6 reported in the specialist press break down into three trim levels.

| Xpeng G6 version | Drivetrain | Indicative price |
|---|---|---|
| Standard | Rear-wheel drive (RWD) | €42,990 |
| Extended range | Rear-wheel drive (RWD) | €46,990 |
| Performance | All-wheel drive (AWD) | €50,990 |

These amounts, before any ecological bonus since it does not apply to the Xpeng G6, compare with very close Tesla Model Y prices on some versions. The decisive difference therefore does not lie in the listed catalogue price, but in access to public incentives. **The Xpeng G6, assembled in China, does not qualify for the French ecological bonus**, unlike the Tesla Model Y built in Germany when it meets the current price criteria. This gap, which can amount to several thousand euros depending on household income, must be factored into any final budget comparison.

## Dealer network, after-sales service and test drives before buying

Choosing a Chinese electric SUV is not just about comparing spec sheets. Dealer network density, parts availability and breakdown response times vary noticeably between a brand recently established in France and a long-standing manufacturer. These questions deserve to be asked before signing, particularly for a model whose French commercial presence remains younger than that of the Tesla Model Y.

Our [Geely dealer guide]({{< relref "geely-dealer.md" >}}) details the method for checking a young after-sales network, an approach that can be applied to any Chinese brand recently arrived in the country. The manufacturer's warranty deserves the same attention: duration, mileage, covered components and the conditions for handling a battery-specific part do not necessarily match the standards already known from mainstream brands.

A test drive covering the usual route remains, in every case, the only way to confirm that a spec sheet matches real-world use; no figure replaces this personal check before a financial commitment of this size.
